This slide is about what MCP means, not how it works. Two ways to tell it, one for each half of the room. For the developers: do the maths on the old way. Five AI products, five internal systems — that's twenty-five separate integrations to write and maintain. With MCP it's five servers, and every agent you add later gets all of them for free. Our ledger is nothing special here; it just sits on that network next to Jira and Confluence. Worth stressing the middle card, because people hear "tools" and stop there. A server can just as easily hand over knowledge: a glossary of your domain terms, a product catalogue, the history of what the team already tried. The model was trained on the general world — the server is how it learns about yours. Our ledger is exactly that kind of server: it gives the agents the project's memory, not a new ability. For the stakeholders: the last card and the closing line are the real point. This is as much about control as it is about capability. The model can be as clever as it likes — it can still only do the things we handed it, and each of those checks its input. New abilities come from plugging in more servers; the control stays in one place. That's what makes it safe to point at real project data. And the quiet punchline for both: it's an open standard, so none of this is locked to one vendor or one editor. The tools will change; the work you put in here survives that.
