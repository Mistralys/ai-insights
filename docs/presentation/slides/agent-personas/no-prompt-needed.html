The Craft of Persona Curation

No Prompt Required

A persona that declares its inputs knows what to do with a file you hand it.

Without a persona

"Here is a plan document. Please read it, identify the work packages, check whether the sequencing makes sense, and…"

With the Planner persona

path/to/plan.md

(That's the whole message.)

The Inputs section tells the model what it may receive.
Recognising the input is the instruction.

Drop a synthesis.md on the Planner and it plans the follow-up work.
Drop it on the Knowledge Archiver and it extracts the lessons. Same file, different specialist.

This is the payoff for writing an explicit Inputs section — and it's the moment most people realise personas are not just longer prompts. Once the persona declares what it accepts, you stop writing instructions and start handing over material. It feels less like operating a tool and more like delegating to someone who already knows the job. Worth saying out loud for the managers in the room: this is exactly how you'd brief a colleague. You don't explain the process every time — you pass them the document.
