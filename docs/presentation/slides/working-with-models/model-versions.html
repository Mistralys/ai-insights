Newer Is Not Simply Better

A new model version is a new colleague, not a promotion for the old one.

Adjusting your personas is expected and encouraged. They are living documents, and fully yours to maintain.

Within the same version, models also evolve as providers work on them continuously.

This one costs people real time, so it's worth stating plainly: a version number going up does not mean every behaviour got better. It means you have a different colleague now. The example is ours and it's recent. The Plan Auditor is deliberately scoped to audit and not to rewrite — that separation is what keeps the audit trustworthy. Older models read that as a strong preference and would still make a correction if you asked them to. Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 read the same sentence as a hard constraint and refused, including on direct instruction to fix a defect they had just reported. Note what happened there. Nothing regressed. The model got better at doing exactly what the persona said. Our wording was just looser than we thought it was, and the older model's leniency had been quietly covering for it. So the practical advice is: when you move to a new model, watch the first few real runs. Benchmarks won't tell you this — it only shows up against your personas and your project. And when you find something, adjust the persona. That's not a failure of the design, that's the design working. The persona is a living document; it's versioned and built from one source precisely so that this kind of change is a single edit rather than a hunt through copies. If anyone asks whether that means pinning model versions: you can, and for a production pipeline it's reasonable. But you'll want to move eventually, and the same audit is waiting for you then.
